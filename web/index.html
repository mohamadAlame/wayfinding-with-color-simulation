Find a building

You'll walk through two small 3D neighbourhoods and find a building in each. It takes about 8 minutes, on a phone or a computer. The directions are in Lebanese Arabizi (like "awal mafra2 3al yamin").

  1. A short practice, to learn how to move.
  2. Two walks. Each time you get directions once, then find the building.
  3. A few quick questions.

Before you start

  • This is a student project for a Human-Centered Design course at AUB, about how people find their way. We are testing the directions, not you.
  • We record your answers, the route you walk and where you look in the 3D street (so we can replay it), how long it takes, which buildings you choose, and whether you use a phone or a computer. No name, email, camera or microphone.
  • It's voluntary. You can stop at any time by closing the page; what was recorded until then is kept unless you ask us to delete it.
  • You get a code, . With it, we can delete your data later. At the end we explain exactly what we were looking at, and you can delete it yourself.

Practice

This doesn't count. It's only to learn how to move.

  • Look around: drag the picture.
  • Walk: hold Walk, or tap a white arrow on the road.
  • Keyboard: W or ↑ walks, S or ↓ steps back, A and D (or ← →) turn.
  • Finish: stand in front of the building, put the white dot on it, and press This is the building.

Practice directions. Read them now: like in the real walks, they disappear once you start.

What we were looking at

The buildings had large posters (banners). In one of your walks they all looked the same, as they do today. In the other each building's poster had its own colour, and the directions mentioned it. In that walk a line of each poster's colour was also painted on the road, leading to its building. We want to know whether that makes a building easier to find.
